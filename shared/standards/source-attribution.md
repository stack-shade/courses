# Source and Attribution Guide

The courses should teach in original words and use external sources to verify and deepen understanding.

## Source priority

1. Standards, RFCs, specifications and research papers.
2. Official product and language documentation.
3. First-party engineering blogs describing real production systems.
4. Academic course notes and textbooks.
5. High-quality independent explainers, used as secondary context.

## How to cite a source

Near the claim or in a Sources section, link the title, publisher / author where available, and the specific page or paper. For version-sensitive material, add the version and date checked.

When using a source to support a conclusion, explain which part it supports. A collection of links without context is not a bibliography.

## Copyright and licensing

- Summarize technical ideas in your own words.
- Do not copy articles, book chapters, paid course content, slides, screenshots or diagrams.
- Keep short quotations clearly marked and properly attributed.
- Check the license before reusing source code, diagrams, data or images.
- Link to external sources instead of republishing them.
- Mark original course assets and code with their applicable license.

## Evidence and uncertainty

Mark assumptions and hypotheses. Separate measured results from estimates. For a production engineering post, capture the workload, context, measured outcome and limitations; do not generalize one company's solution into a universal rule.
